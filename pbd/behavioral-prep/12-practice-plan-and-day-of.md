# 12. Practice Plan, Mocks, Rubric, and Interview Day

Reading this course won't make you good at behavioral interviews. **Saying your stories out loud, many times, under some pressure, will.** This module is the training system: a 3-week plan, the rehearsal protocol, a self-scoring rubric, mock scripts, a tracker, and the day-of checklist.

---

## At a glance

| Goal by the end | How you'll know |
|---|---|
| 14 stories drafted with no brackets in your top 8 | `04-story-bank.md` worksheet complete |
| Top 8 stories at 30 s / 2–3 min / 5 min, without notes | Recorded and timed |
| Any question from modules 05–10 → story picked in ≤ 5 seconds | Rapid-fire drill ≥ 90% |
| Survive 4 levels of follow-up on your top 6 stories | Mock interviewer couldn't break them |
| Average rubric score ≥ 4 on every dimension | Two consecutive mocks |
| Pitch, "why leaving," "why us" smooth for each target company | Recorded once per company |

## 2. Rehearsal protocol (every story)

1. **Beats on paper first:** headline, 2–4 decisions with alternatives, number, learning. One index card.
2. **Say it out loud with a timer.** Not in your head. Your mouth has to learn it.
3. **Stop at 3 minutes** even if you're not done. Note where the time went.
4. **Ask yourself the 8 recurring follow-ups** (`03-storytelling-framework.md` §5). Any you stumble on, write the answer on the card.
5. **Re-tell immediately**, aiming for tighter.
6. **Spaced repetition:** re-tell each top story on day +1, +3, +7, and the day before each interview.

**Recording:** record at least three times during prep (week 1, after mock #1, before your first real loop). Watch for: "we" vs "I," filler words, the first 20 seconds (did you headline?), and the last 10 seconds (did you stop cleanly?).

**Don't memorize scripts.** If you notice yourself reciting identical sentences, change the order of the beats on your next pass. You want fluency, not a recital.

## 3. Self-scoring rubric

Score every mock and every recording, 1–5 per dimension. These dimensions mirror what interviewers write about you (`01-rounds-and-how-youre-evaluated.md`).

| Dimension | 1 | 3 | 5 |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Structure** | Rambling, chronological, no headline | STAR present but uneven; long setup | Headline first; clear beats; ~2–3 min; clean hook |
| **Scope** | Task-level, own team only | Project-level, a few teams | Org-level or cross-org; ambiguous problem you framed |
| **Ownership ("I")** | "We" throughout; role unclear | Role clear but decisions blurry | 3+ decisions clearly yours; credits the team accurately |
| **Judgment** | One option presented as obvious | Mentions an alternative | Options, criteria, tradeoff accepted, when you'd decide differently |
| **Influence** | No other people in the story | Others agreed with you | A real skeptic; you understood their incentive; something specific changed their mind |
| **Impact** | No result | Result without a number or "so what" | Quantified result and business meaning |
| **Self-awareness** | No learning, or a platitude | Generic learning | Concrete mechanism you changed; honest about mistakes |
| **Follow-up depth** | Story collapses on probe 2 | Holds 2 levels | Holds 4 levels with specifics, including the other side's view |
| **Communication** | Monologue; filler; reads notes | Mostly clear | Concise, conversational, checks in, adapts to the interviewer |

**Readiness bar:** ≥ 4 on every dimension in two consecutive mocks. Scope and Judgment are the two that decide Staff vs Senior, so fix those first.

## 4. Rapid-fire drill (story selection speed)

Put every question heading from modules 05–11 into a list (or use the "Rapid-fire variants" tables). Then:

1. Pick 20 at random.
2. For each: **5 seconds** to name the story number, then a **60-second** answer (headline + one decision + result).
3. Score: correct story choice? Headline in the first sentence? Under 60 s?

**Target:** 18/20 on story choice, and no question where you can't name a story within 5 seconds. Questions that stump you go into the tracker (§6) and get a second story assigned.

---

## 5. Follow-up gauntlet

Have a partner (or yourself, recorded) drill one story for 10 minutes with only follow-up questions, getting deeper each time:

1. Why that approach?
2. What did you specifically do?
3. Who disagreed? What exactly did they say?
4. What would they say about how you handled it?
5. What would you do differently?
6. What if [a key constraint] had been different?
7. What happened six months later?
8. What did your manager think?
9. What was the cost of your decision? Who paid it?
10. What's something about this you're not proud of?

**Pass:** you answered all 10 with specifics, without contradicting yourself, and without inventing.

## 7. Mock interviews

### Who to ask
- **Best:** a Staff+ engineer or EM at a target-tier company. Fellow Karmada maintainers or KubeCon peers (`../interview-playbook.md` §9).
- **Good:** a senior peer who has interviewed candidates.
- **Paid platforms:** worth it for at least one Staff behavioral mock if peers aren't available.

### Mock format (give this to your interviewer)

> "Please run a 45-minute Staff-level behavioral interview. Ask 4–5 questions from different areas (leadership/influence, conflict, failure, growing others, ambiguity). For each, follow up at least three levels deep: push on what I specifically did, who disagreed, and what I'd do differently. At the end, tell me: would you hire at Staff, Senior, or no? What was the weakest answer and why? Then score me on the rubric I'll send."

Send them §3 (rubric) and §5 (follow-up gauntlet) in advance.

### Mock question sets

Rotate so you don't see the same set twice.

| Set A | Set B | Set C |
|---|---|---|
| Tell me about yourself | Walk me through your current role | Why are you leaving? |
| Hardest technical decision you've made | Influenced a decision without authority | A time you led across multiple teams |
| A time you disagreed with a peer | A time you disagreed with your manager | Time you said no to a stakeholder |
| Biggest failure | A decision you'd make differently | An incident you owned |
| How have you grown other engineers? | Giving hard feedback | Someone who was struggling |
| Questions for me | Questions for me | Questions for me |

**EM-flavored mock** (for HM and team-match rounds): "What would you want to own in your first 6 months?" · "How do you work with an EM?" · "How do you decide what *not* to work on?" · "How would your current manager describe you?" · "What kind of team do you do your best work on?"

## 8. The 48 hours before an interview

| When | Do |
|---|---|
| **48 h before** | Research the company and team: their infra problem, recent blog posts/talks, open-source work. Write two sentences of "why this company" (`10-motivation-career-and-about-you.md`). Ask the recruiter who you're meeting and what each round covers |
| **24 h before** | Choose the 6–8 stories most relevant to this company (`04-story-bank.md` §5). Re-tell each once out loud. Prepare 3 questions per interviewer type (`10`). Re-read the company-style notes (`01`) |
| **Evening before** | Cue cards ready (one line per story: headline · number · learning). Test camera, mic, and network. Stop prepping by early evening |
| **Morning of** | Tell your pitch once out loud. Tell your failure and conflict stories once each. Light warm-up only, don't cram |

## 9. Interview day checklist

**Setup (virtual)**
- [ ] Camera at eye level, good light, quiet room, plain background
- [ ] Wired internet or a strong connection; phone hotspot as backup
- [ ] Cue cards off-screen; water nearby
- [ ] Interviewer names and roles written down
- [ ] Personal laptop and accounts only (`../interview-playbook.md` §10)

**In every round**
- [ ] Open with energy; thank them by name
- [ ] Pause before answering: "Let me think of the best example."
- [ ] Headline first; 2–3 minutes; end with a hook
- [ ] "I" for decisions, "we" for team execution
- [ ] One number per story
- [ ] Name a rejected alternative and a skeptic when relevant
- [ ] Honest about limits (GPU/AI-infra): "I haven't done X; here's how I'd approach it"
- [ ] Check in: "Want more detail, or should I move on?"
- [ ] Leave 5+ minutes for your questions; ask 2–3
- [ ] Close: one sentence on why you're excited about *their* problem

**Between rounds**
- [ ] Write down which stories you used and with whom (avoid telling the same story to two interviewers when you have an equally good alternative)
- [ ] Reset: water, stand up, one deep breath. Don't replay the last round

## 10. After every interview

**Within 1 hour, write down:**
- Questions asked (verbatim as best you can)
- Stories used and how each landed
- Follow-ups you struggled with
- Anything they said about the team, its problems, level, or next steps

**Within 24 hours:**
- Thank-you note to the recruiter (and the HM if you have their email), mentioning one specific thing from the conversation, and restating level interest in one line if relevant
- Update the question log (§6) and patch any weak story before the next interview

**After a rejection:** ask the recruiter for feedback ("Is there anything you can share about what would have made me a stronger candidate?"). Many won't share details, but some will tell you about level or which area fell short. Log it.

## 11. Self-check

1. Which two rubric dimensions were lowest in your last mock? What's your fix for each?
2. Have you recorded yourself at least three times?
3. Can you name a story for any question in the rapid-fire list within 5 seconds?
4. Which story has never been through the follow-up gauntlet?
5. For your next interview: which 6–8 stories, which 3 questions per interviewer, and what's your "why this company"?
