# Behavioral Interview Prep: Stories, Not Scripts

A course for passing the behavioral side of Staff-level interviews (recruiter screen → hiring manager → behavioral loop → bar raiser → EM, director, and team-match chats) at platform and AI-infrastructure companies. It's built on **your real work**: Bloomberg's managed multi-cluster platform, Karmada, and the community around it.

Companion to [`../coding-prep/`](../coding-prep/README.md). The full interview process, including system design and negotiation, is in [`../interview-playbook.md`](../interview-playbook.md).

---

## Philosophy

1. **Behavioral rounds decide your level.** Coding and design show you can do the work. Behavioral rounds show the *scope* you work at. Most Staff-vs-Senior decisions come down to them.
2. **About 15 real stories cover nearly every question.** You don't prepare answers to 200 questions. You prepare 15 stories well and learn to map any question onto them.
3. **Structure is what makes you credible.** Headline first, then decisions with alternatives, a quantified result, and a concrete learning (STAR-L). Same content, better structure, higher level.
4. **Follow-ups are the real interview.** The first answer earns attention. Four levels of "why" and "what exactly did you do" decide the outcome. Prepare one level deeper than feels necessary.
5. **Never invent.** Every `[bracket]` in this course is a detail only you can supply. Interviewers drill until made-up details fall apart.
6. **Say it out loud.** Reading prep feels productive but doesn't transfer. Rehearse aloud, record yourself, and do mocks.

## How each question module is structured (05–10)

1. **At a glance:** each question family, what it's really testing, and your best stories for it.
2. **What Staff looks like here:** Senior vs Staff answers and the anti-signals.
3. **Question clusters**, each with:
   - every common phrasing, so you recognize the question however it's worded
   - what they're really asking
   - which stories to use (with a backup)
   - an answer skeleton with a time split
   - a sample answer from your real work, in a collapsible block so you can try first
   - likely follow-ups and how to handle them
   - traps
4. **Rapid-fire variants:** more phrasings mapped to clusters and stories.
5. **Self-check and drill.**

## How to use this course

1. **Read 02 and 03 first.** They change how you tell every story.
2. **Build your story bank (04) before anything else.** Start from the drafts in **13**, then replace the invented details with real ones. Start with **Story #11 (failure)** and **Story #12 (conflict)**. They're asked in nearly every loop, and they're the two that need real events from you.
3. **Work through 05–10** by speaking answers out loud, timed, and then answering the listed follow-ups.
4. **Read 01 and 11** before your first real screen, so you know what each round wants and how to recover when things go sideways.
5. **Follow the plan in 12:** three weeks, two mocks minimum, rubric scores of 4+ before your top-choice loops.
6. **Before each interview:** pick 6–8 stories for that company (04 §5), prepare questions (10), and run the 48-hour routine (12 §8).

## Confidentiality

Use public framing only: your talks, the CNCF reference architecture, and Karmada GitHub. Refer to colleagues by role, not name. Describe internal systems generically ("an internal token exchange service"). "I can't share the specifics, but the shape of the problem was…" is a judgment signal, not a dodge.
